5.1 Lectures

To ensure students have the required background in plant functional and climate change ecology and the methodologies, students are assigned readings before the course (Table 1). During earlier iterations, readings were merely assigned, but as the course evolved we shifted to a more active model where students read papers and then discuss them within their groups. Readings include foundational papers for trait-based ecology, background on the study region, and at least one paper that helps understand the work going on in each group. In addition to these general readings, students are assigned several papers focused on their particular group.

Background on the places and experiments is in Alpine ecosystems, the Three-D project, and the INCLINE project. The reading records are in course_papers.bib. Add a paper there, then add its key to Table 1.

5.2 Online lectures

We include lectures on core subjects as part of each course (Table 2). During our earlier courses, these were included in the beginning of each course. However, we found that this was a sub-optimal approach, as it cut into time available for field work and analysis, and participants were often suffering from severe jet lag. In response, we now hold the background lectures online in the months before the course, increasing time in the field and giving the students the necessary background information sooner (Geange et al., 2021).

5.3 Pre-course discussions

In addition to pre-course readings and lectures, later iterations of the course included virtual sessions where groups met to discuss readings, design their studies, and begin writing data documentation. Initial iterations had pre-defined research projects, but in later iterations we’ve involved students to a greater extent in the planning phase. By allowing the students to direct the research (within certain constraints), our goal is to give them a greater sense of agency and ownership of their project, as well as exposing them to more of the data life cycle, from planning all the way to publication. This phase of the course utilizes a variety of online tools, including email, Slack, Google Docs, Github, and Zoom/Skype/Teams.
